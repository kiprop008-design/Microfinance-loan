# Loan Repayment Analysis: Mombasa Microfinance Institution

An analysis of loan sizes and repayment timing, prepared for a board presentation.

> **Note:** All data in this repository is **fictional**. The 150 loan records were generated by `loan_analysis.py` (fixed random seed, so results are reproducible). Replace `loans.csv` with real data before drawing any conclusions about actual borrowers.

## Key findings for the board

**Correlation, in plain language:**

> Larger loans tend to take slightly longer to receive their first repayment, but the link is weak: loan size explains only about 6% of the variation in repayment timing, so the size of a loan alone is not a reliable way to predict who will pay late.

**Typical loan size.** The typical (median) loan is **KES 38,750**. The average is higher at KES 48,683 because a handful of very large loans pull it upward, so the median is the fairer picture of a "normal" loan. Half of all loans fall between **KES 28,000 and KES 55,125**.

**Unusually large or small loans.** Seven loans are statistically unusual, and all of them are large (above KES 95,812): **LN0018, LN0031, LN0089, LN0119, LN0122, LN0139, LN0140**. The largest is KES 365,000, roughly nine times the typical loan. No loans were flagged as unusually small. The smallest loans (LN0045 at KES 2,500 and LN0104 at KES 3,000) are tiny but not flagged, because in a dataset where most loans are small, the method's lower cutoff falls below zero.

**Repayment timing.** The first repayment typically arrives about **31 days** after disbursement, and most loans (the middle 50%) fall between 27 and 34 days.

## Outliers (IQR method)

## Correlation (technical detail)

| Measure | Value | p-value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pearson r | +0.251 | 0.0020 |
| Spearman r | +0.243 | 0.0027 |

Both measures agree: a weak positive relationship that is statistically significant (unlikely to be chance), but with r² ≈ 0.06. Spearman is included as a check because it is not distorted by the large outlier loans. Correlation does not show that large loans *cause* slower repayment.
